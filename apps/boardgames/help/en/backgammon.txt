Backgammon
# The aim
Bring your fifteen checkers round into your home board, the quarter on your side by the tray, then bear them off. The first to bear off all fifteen wins. The rules below are those of backgammon; plakoto, fevga and long nardy come after them.
# Throwing and moving
Each player throws one die to start; the higher plays first with both numbers. Then throw two dice and move by each number, with one checker or two.
- Land on an empty point, on your own checkers, or on a single enemy checker.
- A double gives four moves.
- Use both numbers if you can, else the larger. With no move, the turn is lost.
# Hitting and entering
Landing on a single enemy checker sends it to the bar. A checker on the bar must enter before anything else moves, in the other player's home board, on the point of a die (the 1 is furthest from the bar). A point held by two or more enemy checkers is closed.
# Bearing off
Once all fifteen are home, move a checker to the tray: a number bears off from its point. With that point empty, move from a higher point; with none higher, bear off from your highest.
# Winning
One point; two (gammon) if the loser has borne off none; three (backgammon) if they also have a checker on the bar or in the winner's home board. No doubling cube.
# Plakoto
All checkers start on your 24 point; you move in opposite directions. No hitting.
- Landing on a single enemy checker pins it until your checker leaves.
- Your last checker on your 24 point is the mother. If it is pinned there, its player loses two points at once, unless the pinner still has checkers on their own 24 point: the game ends once they have none there. Both mothers pinned: a draw.
- The opening throw's winner throws again to start. One point, or two if the loser has borne off none.
# Fevga
All checkers start on your 24 point, in opposite corners, and both players move the same way. No hitting: a single checker holds a point.
- Your first checker must pass the other player's start before a second leaves yours.
- You may not hold all six points of your starting quarter, nor six in a row with all fifteen enemy checkers behind them.
- Opening throw and scoring as in plakoto.
# Long nardy
As in fevga, from the head (your 24 point).
- One checker leaves the head each turn; on your first throw only, 6-6, 4-4 or 3-3 lets two leave.
- No six points in a row unless an enemy checker is already past them.
- Opening throw as in plakoto. One point (oyn), or two (mars) if the loser has borne off none.
