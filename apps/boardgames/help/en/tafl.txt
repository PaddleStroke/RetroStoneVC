Hnefatafl
# The aim
The defenders must help their king escape. The attackers, twice as many, must capture him. The attackers (dark) move first.
# How the pieces move
- Every piece, the king too, moves like a chess rook: any number of empty squares along a row or a column.
- Only the king may stop on the middle square, the throne. Any piece may pass over it when it is empty.
- In Hnefatafl, only the king may stop on the four corners.
# Capturing
- A piece is captured when an enemy moves so that it is caught between two enemies along a row or a column. One move can capture up to three pieces.
- The corners and the empty throne act as enemies of every piece. The king helps the defenders capture.
- A piece may safely move into the gap between two enemies.
# The king
- The king is captured by attackers on all four of his sides, or on three with the empty throne on the fourth. He cannot be captured on the edge.
- The defenders win when the king reaches a corner.
# Tablut (9 by 9)
- The corners are ordinary squares. The king wins on any square of the edge.
- Away from the throne and its neighbours, two attackers on opposite sides capture the king.
# Brandubh (7 by 7)
- The corners and the throne are as in Hnefatafl.
- Away from the throne and its neighbours, the king is captured like any piece: by two attackers on opposite sides, or by one with a corner on his other side.
# The end of the game
- A player who cannot move loses.
- The same position for the third time with the same player to move, or 1000 moves each, is a draw.
- Copenhagen rules, without shieldwall, edge fort and encirclement.
