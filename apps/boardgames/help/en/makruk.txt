Makruk
# The aim
Thai chess: checkmate the other Khun (king). White moves first.
# How the pieces move
- Khun (the crown): one square in any direction. There is no castling.
- Met (the diamond): one square diagonally.
- Khon (the bell): one square diagonally, or one square straight forwards.
- Ma (the horse): like a chess knight.
- Rua (the tower): like a chess rook.
- Bia (the shell): one square straight forwards, never two. It captures one square diagonally forwards. On the sixth row it becomes a Met.
# Check and the end of the game
You may never make a move that leaves your own Khun in check.
- Checkmate: the Khun is in check and no move can save it. That player loses.
- Draw: stalemate (no legal move, but not in check), only the two Khun left, or the same position for the third time with the same player to move.
# Counting
When no unpromoted Bia is left, the game must end soon. The device counts for you.
- The board's count: the player who moves next counts their moves. If the count reaches 64 and the other player does not checkmate with the next move, the game is a draw.
- The pieces' count: when a player has only their Khun left, they count instead, from the number of pieces on the board (both Khun included) plus one. The other player must checkmate before the count passes the limit: 8 with two Rua, 16 with one Rua, 22 with two Khon, 32 with two Ma, 44 with one Khon, else 64. The limit is set when the count starts.
