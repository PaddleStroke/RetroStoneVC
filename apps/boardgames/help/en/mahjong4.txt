Mahjong
# The aim
The classic game for four, by the Hong Kong rules (Old Style). Be the first to complete a hand of 14 tiles worth at least 3 faan.
# The tiles
- Three suits, 1 to 9: dots, bamboo (the 1 is a bird) and characters (a Chinese numeral above 萬).
- The honours: the winds 東 east, 南 south, 西 west, 北 north; the dragons 中 red, 發 green and white (a blue frame).
- Four of each. The flowers and seasons are bonus tiles: put aside at once and replaced from the end of the wall.
# A turn
13 tiles each, the dealer (east) 14. Draw from the wall or claim the last discard, then discard a tile. Before discarding you may declare a win, or a kong (four of a kind in your hand, or the fourth tile added to a pung you laid down) and draw a replacement.
# Sets and claims
- Chow: three in a row of one suit. Pung: three of a kind. Kong: four of a kind. Pair: two of a kind.
- Anyone may claim the last discard for a pung, a kong or a win; only the next player for a chow. The set is laid face up, the claimer discards, and play goes on after them.
- A win beats a pung or a kong, which beats a chow. Between two wins, the first after the discarder takes it.
- A tile added to a pung can be robbed by a player who wins with it.
- Once the wall is used up, the last discard can only be claimed for a win; otherwise the hand is a draw.
# Winning
Four sets and a pair, seven different pairs, or thirteen orphans (one of each 1, 9 and honour, plus one more of them).
# Faan
- 1: self-drawn; concealed hand; all chows; each pung of dragons; a pung of your seat wind or of the prevailing wind; each of your own flower and season (east 1, south 2, west 3, north 4); no flowers or seasons; winning with the last tile, a replacement tile, or by robbing a kong.
- 2: all four flowers, or all four seasons.
- 3: all pungs; half flush (one suit and honours).
- 4: seven pairs.
- 5: small three dragons (two pungs of dragons and a pair of the third), instead of the dragon pungs.
- 7: full flush (one suit only).
- 8: big three dragons.
- 10, the limit: thirteen orphans, all honours, all 1s and 9s, small or big four winds, nine gates (1112345678999 of one suit plus one more), four kongs, four concealed pungs.
A hand counts the best way it can be read, up to 10 faan.
# Payments
3 to 10 faan are worth 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 or 128 points. On a discard, the discarder pays twice that and the others once; self-drawn, all three pay twice.
# The end of the game
The dealer keeps the deal by winning; otherwise it passes on. When everyone has dealt, the prevailing wind turns from east to south. After the last round, the highest total wins.
