Spades
# The aim
Two sides of two partners, sitting face to face. Each player bids how many tricks they will take; then the sides try to make their bids. The first side to reach 500 points wins.
# Bidding
13 cards each. In turn, each player bids from 1 to 13 tricks, or nil: to take no trick at all. The two partners' bids add up to their side's contract.
# Playing
- The first bidder leads. Follow the suit led if you can, otherwise play any card.
- Spades are always trumps: the highest spade wins the trick, or else the highest card of the suit led. The ace is high. The winner leads next.
- Spades may not be led until a spade has been played on another suit, unless the leader holds only spades.
# Scoring
- Contract made (by the partners who did not bid nil): 10 points per trick bid, and 1 per extra trick, called a bag.
- Contract lost: minus 10 per trick bid.
- Nil: 100 if its bidder takes no trick, minus 100 otherwise. A nil bidder's tricks never count for the contract; they count as bags.
- Each time a side has 10 bags, it loses 100 points and those bags are gone.
# The end of the game
After the round in which a side reaches 500 points, the side with more points wins; if both have the same score, another round is played.
