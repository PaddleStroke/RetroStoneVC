Chess
# The aim
Checkmate the other king: attack it so that it cannot escape. White moves first.
# How the pieces move
- King: one square in any direction. Queen: any number of squares along a row, a column or a diagonal. Rook: along a row or a column. Bishop: along a diagonal.
- Knight: an L shape, two squares one way and one to the side. It is the only piece that jumps over others.
- Pawn: one square forward, or two from its starting square. It captures one square diagonally forward.
- A piece captures by moving onto an enemy piece.
# Special moves
- Castling: the king moves two squares towards a rook, and the rook jumps to the square the king crossed. Only if neither has moved, nothing stands between them, and the king is not in check, does not cross an attacked square and does not land in check.
- En passant: a pawn that has just moved two squares, landing beside an enemy pawn, may be taken by it as if it had moved one square, on the very next move only.
- Promotion: a pawn reaching the last row becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight.
# Check and the end of the game
You may never make a move that leaves your own king in check (attacked).
- Checkmate: the king is in check and no move can save it. That player loses.
- Draw: stalemate (no legal move, but not in check), too few pieces left to checkmate, the same position for the third time, or fifty moves each without a capture or a pawn move.
# Chess960
The back-row pieces start in a random order, the bishops on opposite colours and the king between the rooks. Castling ends as in chess (the king on g and the rook on f, or the king on c and the rook on d), and every square they cross or land on must be empty apart from them. To castle, move the king onto its rook.
# Crazyhouse
A piece you capture changes sides and goes into your hand. Instead of moving, you may drop a piece from your hand on any empty square, even to give check or mate. No pawn on the first or last row. A captured promoted piece returns as a pawn. No draw for lack of pieces.
# Three-check
The first player to give check three times wins. Checkmate also wins.
# King of the Hill
The first king to reach one of the four centre squares wins. Checkmate also wins.
# Losing chess
The aim is to lose all your pieces: you win when you have none left, or cannot move.
- Capturing is compulsory, but you may choose which capture.
- The king is an ordinary piece: no check, it can be captured, no castling. A pawn may also promote to a king.
- Only bishops left, each side's on squares of a different colour: a draw.
# Atomic
Every capture explodes: the captured piece, the capturing piece and every piece except pawns on the eight squares around leave the board.
- Blowing up the other king wins, even when your own king is in check. Checkmate also wins.
- A king may not capture, and no move may blow up your own king. Two kings side by side are never in check.
- A draw when neither side has enough pieces left to win.
# Racing Kings
No pawns, and no move may give check. The first king to reach the last row wins; if the white king gets there first and the black king follows on the very next move, it is a draw.
# Horde
White has 36 pawns and no king, so it is never in check. White wins by checkmate, Black by capturing every white piece. White's pawns on the first row may also move two squares (they cannot be taken en passant then).
