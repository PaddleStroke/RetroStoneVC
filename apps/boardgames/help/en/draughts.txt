Draughts
# The aim
Capture all the other player's pieces, or leave them without a move. Pieces stand on the dark squares and move diagonally, except in Turkish draughts.
# International draughts (10 by 10)
- Light moves first. Men move one square diagonally forward, and capture forwards and backwards by jumping over an enemy piece next to them onto the empty square just beyond.
- Capturing is compulsory: take the largest number of pieces possible, jumping on in the same move. The pieces taken are removed at the end of the move, and none is jumped twice.
- A man that ends its move on the far row becomes a king. Kings move any number of squares diagonally, and capture from a distance, landing on any empty square beyond.
- Draw: the same position for the third time, or 25 moves each with only kings moving and no capture.
# English draughts (8 by 8)
- Dark moves first. Men move and capture forwards only. Kings move and capture one square diagonally, in any direction.
- Capturing is compulsory, but you may choose which capture. A piece that starts jumping must take every piece it can. Becoming a king ends the move.
- Draw: the same position for the third time, or 40 moves each without a capture or a man moving.
# Russian draughts (8 by 8)
- As international draughts, but you may choose which capture. A piece that starts jumping must take every piece it can.
- After a capture, a king must land on a square from which it can go on capturing, if there is one.
- A man reaching the far row becomes a king at once, even in the middle of a capture, and goes on capturing as a king.
- Draw: the same position for the third time, or 15 moves each with only kings moving and no capture.
# Brazilian draughts (8 by 8)
International draughts on an 8 by 8 board. Draw: the same position for the third time, or 20 moves each with only kings moving and no capture.
# Turkish draughts (8 by 8)
- All 64 squares are used: 16 men each, on their second and third rows. Light moves first.
- Men move and capture one square straight forward or sideways. Kings move any number of squares along a row or a column, and capture from a distance.
- Capturing is compulsory: take the largest number of pieces possible. Each piece taken is removed at once, and the capturing piece may not turn straight back.
- Draw: the same position for the third time, or 25 moves each without a capture or a man moving.
# Frisian draughts (10 by 10)
- International draughts, but men and kings also capture straight along the rows and columns, over an enemy piece two squares away onto the dark square just beyond. Kings capture from a distance in all eight directions.
- You must take the most valuable pieces: a king is worth more than one man but less than two. When a king and a man can take the same value, the king must capture.
- While you still have men, the same king may not make more than three moves in a row without capturing.
- Draw: two kings against one after seven moves each, one king against one after two moves each, or the same position for the third time.
# Canadian draughts (12 by 12)
International draughts on a 12 by 12 board, with 30 men each.
# Bashni (8 by 8)
Russian draughts, but the pieces taken stay in the game.
- A capture takes only the top piece of the column it jumps, and puts it under the capturing column when the move ends. The pieces beneath stay, commanded by their new top piece.
- A column belongs to the player whose piece is on top, and moves as that piece does. Only the top piece is crowned. A column can be jumped only once in a capture.
- A player who cannot move loses, even with pieces held under the other's columns. Draws as in Russian draughts.
# The end of the game
A player who has no pieces left, or cannot move, loses.
