Draughts
# The aim
Capture all the other player's pieces, or leave them without a move. Choose the rules before the game: international draughts on a 10 by 10 board, English draughts (checkers), Russian, Brazilian or Turkish draughts on an 8 by 8 board. Pieces stand on the dark squares only and move diagonally, except in Turkish draughts.
# International draughts (10 by 10)
- Light moves first. A man moves one square diagonally forward.
- A man captures by jumping over an enemy piece next to it onto the empty square just beyond. Men capture forwards and backwards.
- Capturing is compulsory, and you must take the largest number of pieces possible. After a jump, if another capture is possible from where the piece lands, it must go on jumping in the same move.
- The pieces taken are removed at the end of the move. A piece cannot be jumped twice.
- A man that ends its move on the far row becomes a king. A king moves any number of squares along a diagonal, and captures from a distance, landing on any empty square beyond the piece it takes.
- Draw: when the same position comes back for the third time, or after 25 moves each with only kings moving and no capture.
# English draughts (8 by 8)
- Dark moves first. Men move and capture forwards only.
- Capturing is compulsory, but you may choose which capture to make. Once a piece starts jumping, it must take every piece it can along its way.
- A man reaching the far row becomes a king, and that ends the move. A king moves and captures one square diagonally in any direction.
- Draw: when the same position comes back for the third time, or after 40 moves each without a capture or a man moving.
# Russian draughts (8 by 8)
- Light moves first. A man moves one square diagonally forward, and captures forwards and backwards.
- Kings move and capture from a distance along the diagonals, as in international draughts. After a capture, a king must land on a square from which it can go on capturing, if there is one.
- Capturing is compulsory, but you may choose which capture to make. Once a piece starts jumping, it must take every piece it can along its way.
- The pieces taken are removed at the end of the move. A piece cannot be jumped twice.
- A man reaching the far row becomes a king at once, even in the middle of a capture, and then goes on capturing as a king if it can.
- Draw: when the same position comes back for the third time, or after 15 moves each with only kings moving and no capture.
# Brazilian draughts (8 by 8)
- The rules of international draughts on an 8 by 8 board. Light moves first.
- Men capture forwards and backwards, kings move and capture from a distance, and you must take the largest number of pieces possible.
- A man becomes a king only if it ends its move on the far row.
- Draw: when the same position comes back for the third time, or after 20 moves each with only kings moving and no capture.
# Turkish draughts (8 by 8)
- All 64 squares are used. Each player starts with 16 men on their second and third rows. Light moves first.
- A man moves one square straight forward or sideways, never diagonally and never back. It captures the same way, jumping over an enemy piece next to it onto the empty square just beyond.
- A man reaching the far row becomes a king. A king moves any number of squares along a row or a column, and captures from a distance, landing on any empty square beyond the piece it takes.
- Capturing is compulsory, and you must take the largest number of pieces possible.
- Each piece taken is removed at once, as it is jumped. In a capture of several pieces, a piece cannot turn straight back the way it came.
- Draw: when the same position comes back for the third time, or after 25 moves each without a capture or a man moving.
# Frisian draughts (10 by 10)
- Light moves first. A man moves one square diagonally forward, as in international draughts.
- Men and kings also capture straight along the rows and columns: over an enemy piece on the next dark square, two squares away, onto the dark square just beyond. Men capture backwards too.
- A king moves any number of squares along a diagonal, and captures from a distance in all eight directions.
- Capturing is compulsory, and you must take the most valuable pieces: a king is worth more than one man but less than two. When a king and a man can take the same value, the king must capture.
- A man that ends its move on the far row becomes a king.
- While you still have men, the same king may not make more than three moves in a row without capturing; then another piece must move.
- Draw: when two kings face one and both players have made seven moves, when one king faces one and both have made two moves, or when the same position comes back for the third time.
# Canadian draughts (12 by 12)
The rules of international draughts on a 12 by 12 board, with 30 men each.
# Bashni (8 by 8)
Russian column draughts: the rules of Russian draughts, but the pieces taken stay in the game.
- A capture takes only the top piece of the column it jumps, and puts it under the capturing column. The pieces left beneath stay where they are, commanded by their new top piece.
- A column belongs to the player whose piece is on top, and moves as that piece does, as a man or as a king. Only the top piece is crowned.
- A column can be jumped only once in a capture. The pieces taken go under the capturing column when the move ends.
- Each bar shows how many of the other player's pieces that player holds in their columns. A player who cannot move loses, even with pieces held under the other's columns.
- Draw: as in Russian draughts.
# The end of the game
A player who has no pieces left, or cannot move, loses.
# Playing on the device
Only the pieces that can move can be picked up. Tap a piece, then the square it lands on. For a capture of several pieces, tap each landing square in turn, or just the last one. The pieces about to be taken fade.
