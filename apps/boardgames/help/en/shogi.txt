Shogi
# The aim
Japanese chess: checkmate the other king. A piece points towards its opponent. Black (sente) moves first.
# How the pieces move
- King (玉, 王): one square in any direction.
- Rook (飛): any number of squares along a row or a column. Bishop (角): along a diagonal.
- Gold (金): one square in any direction except diagonally backwards.
- Silver (銀): one square diagonally, or straight forwards.
- Knight (桂): two squares forwards and one to the side, jumping over any piece in between.
- Lance (香): any number of squares straight forwards.
- Pawn (歩): one square straight forwards. It also captures that way.
# Promotion
The three rows farthest from you are the promotion zone. A piece that moves into, within or out of it may promote: its character turns red.
- The rook becomes a dragon (龍), which may also move one square diagonally. The bishop becomes a horse (馬), which may also move one square straight.
- The silver, the knight, the lance and the pawn become golds (全, 圭, 杏, と). The king and the gold do not promote.
- Promotion is compulsory only when the piece could never move again.
# Captured pieces
A captured piece joins your hand. Instead of moving, you may drop it, unpromoted, on any empty square. Not allowed: a pawn on a column that already has one of your unpromoted pawns; a piece where it could never move; a pawn drop that gives immediate checkmate.
# The end of the game
You may never leave your own king in check.
- Checkmate wins. A player who has no legal move also loses.
- When the same position (board, hands and player to move) comes for the fourth time, the game is a draw. But a player who gave check with every move since then loses.
# Mini shogi
On 5 by 5, without knights and lances. The promotion zone is only the farthest row, and a pawn that reaches it must promote. On the fourth repetition, Black (sente) loses, unless the other player gave check with every move since then: then they lose instead.
