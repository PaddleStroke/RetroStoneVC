French Tarot
# The aim
78 cards: four suits of 14 (jack, knight, queen and king above the 10), 21 trumps and the Excuse. In each deal the taker plays against all the others, the defence. After as many deals as there are players (a cancelled deal does not count), the highest total wins.
# The cards
- The three oudlers are the 21, the Petit (the 1 of trumps) and the Excuse. The more of them the taker wins, the fewer points they need.
- Points: an oudler or a king 4.5, a queen 3.5, a knight 2.5, a jack 1.5, any other card 0.5: 91 in all.
# The deal
- The dog gets 6 cards (3 with five players); each player gets 24 with three players, 18 with four, 15 with five.
- A player whose only trump is the Petit, without the Excuse (the Petit sec), shows it and the deal is cancelled.
# The bidding
From the player after the dealer, each player speaks once: pass, or bid higher than every bid before.
- Take (Petite): the taker gets the dog. Counted once.
- Guard (Garde): the same, counted twice.
- Guard without (Garde sans): the dog, unseen, counts for the taker. Four times.
- Guard against (Garde contre): the dog, unseen, counts for the defence. Six times.
If everyone passes, the next player deals.
# Five players: the called king
Before the dog, the taker calls a king (a queen if they hold all four kings). Its holder is the taker's secret partner until the king is played. If the taker holds it, or it lies in the dog, the taker plays alone.
# The dog
With Take and Guard the dog is shown to everyone. The taker adds it to their hand and puts as many cards aside: never a king or an oudler, and a trump only if there is no other way (it is then shown). The cards put aside count for the taker.
# A trick
- The player after the dealer leads first; the winner of a trick leads the next.
- Follow suit. If you cannot, play a trump; with no trump either, any card.
- When a trump is in the trick, play a higher trump if you can, otherwise a lower one.
- The highest trump wins; with no trump, the highest card of the suit led.
- The Excuse can be played at any time and never wins: its owner keeps it and gives the trick's winner a half-point card instead. If it was led, the next card sets the suit. In the last trick it goes to the trick's winner, unless its side won every trick before: then it wins that trick.
# Poignée
Just before playing their first card, a player may show a single poignée of 10 trumps (13 with three players, 8 with five), a double of 13 (15, 10) or a triple of 15 (18, 13); the Excuse may complete it. It is worth 20, 30 or 40 points to the side that wins the deal.
# The score
- The taker's side needs 56 points with no oudler, 51 with one, 41 with two, 36 with three.
- The deal is worth 25 points plus the difference between the points won and needed (a half point counts as a whole), times 1, 2, 4 or 6 for the bid.
- Petit au bout: the Petit in the last trick is worth 10 points, times the bid's number, to the side that wins that trick.
- A side that wins every trick (a chelem, never announced here) scores 200 more.
- Each defender pays the deal's value to the taker, or receives it. With five players and a partner, the taker takes two shares and the partner one.
