French Tarot
# The aim
The French card game of tarot, with 78 cards: four suits of 14 cards (jack, knight, queen and king above the 10), 21 trumps and the Excuse. In each deal one player, the taker, plays against all the others, the defence. A game lasts as many deals as there are players, and the highest total wins. For 3, 4 or 5 players.
# The cards
- The three oudlers are the 21, the Petit (the 1 of trumps) and the Excuse. The more of them the taker wins, the fewer points they need.
- Points: an oudler or a king 4.5, a queen 3.5, a knight 2.5, a jack 1.5, any other card 0.5. There are 91 points in all.
- At the table the cards are counted in pairs: a king or an oudler with a small card makes 5, a queen with a small card 4, a knight 3, a jack 2, two small cards 1. It comes to the same.
# The deal
- The cards are not shuffled between deals: the tricks are gathered, cut and dealt three at a time, as at a real table.
- The cards are dealt three at a time, round the table, starting with the player after the dealer. The dog gets 6 cards (3 with five players), one at a time, never the first or the last cards.
- Each player gets 24 cards with three players, 18 with four, 15 with five.
- A player whose only trump is the Petit, without the Excuse (the Petit sec), shows it and the deal is cancelled. The next player deals.
# The bidding
Starting with the player after the dealer, each player speaks once: they pass, or bid higher than every bid before.
- Take (Petite): the taker gets the dog. The deal counts once.
- Guard (Garde): the same, counted twice.
- Guard without (Garde sans): nobody sees the dog; its cards count for the taker. Counted four times.
- Guard against (Garde contre): nobody sees the dog; its cards count for the defence. Counted six times.
If everyone passes, the deal is cancelled and the next player deals.
# Five players: the called king
Before the dog, the taker calls a king (a queen if they hold all four kings). Whoever holds it becomes the taker's partner, but keeps it secret until the king is played. If the taker holds it, or if it lies in the dog, the taker plays alone against four.
# The dog
With Take and Guard the dog is turned face up for everyone to see. Then the taker adds it to their hand and secretly puts the same number of cards aside: never a king or an oudler, and a trump only if there is no other way. Trumps put aside are shown. The cards put aside count for the taker.
# A trick
- The player after the dealer leads the first trick; whoever wins a trick leads the next.
- Follow the suit that was led. If you have none of it, you must play a trump; with no trump either, play any card.
- When a trump is in the trick, you must play a higher trump if you can; if you cannot, play a lower one.
- The highest trump wins the trick; with no trump, the highest card of the suit led.
- The Excuse can be played at any time instead and never wins: its owner keeps it and gives the trick's winner a card worth half a point in exchange. If it was led, the next card sets the suit. Played in the last trick, it goes to the trick's winner, unless its side has won every trick before: then it wins that last trick.
# Poignée
A player with many trumps may show them just before playing their first card: a single poignée of 10 trumps (13 with three players, 8 with five), a double of 13 (15, 10) or a triple of 15 (18, 13). The Excuse may complete it. It is worth 20, 30 or 40 points to the side that wins the deal.
# The score
- The taker's side needs 56 points with no oudler, 51 with one, 41 with two, 36 with all three. With those points it wins the deal, without them it loses it.
- The deal is worth 25 points plus the difference between the points won and the points needed (a half point counts as a whole one), times 1, 2, 4 or 6 for the bid.
- Petit au bout: the Petit in the last trick is worth 10 points, times the bid's number, to the side that wins that trick.
- A side that wins every trick (a chelem; it is never announced here) scores 200 more.
- Each defender pays the deal's value to the taker, or receives it from them. With five players and a partner, the taker takes two shares and the partner one. The scores always add up to zero.
# The end of the game
After as many deals as there are players (a cancelled deal does not count), the player with the highest total wins.
# Playing on the device
Pass the device round: the player whose turn it is sees their hand at the bottom, in one or two rows. Each player's bar shows their total, how many cards they hold and their bid. To bid, tap a button. The trick lies in the middle, each card on its player's side, with their token; the trick just won stays there, the winner's card glowing, until the next card is led. Tap the pile at the top left to see the last trick again. When the dog is turned up, everyone sees it while the taker's hand stays face down: show the device round, then the taker taps the dog to take it, taps the cards to put aside (tap one again to take it back) and the round button. When you may show a poignée, a button with its number of trumps appears at the top. After each deal a table shows the bid with its number, the oudlers the taker's side won, its points, the points it needed and the difference, the bonuses, then each player's points and total; tap it for the next deal. Undo takes back only your own moves of this turn.
