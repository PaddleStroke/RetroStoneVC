Egyptian Ratscrew
# The aim
Win all the cards. A fast game of nerves, called Bataille corse in France: everyone plays on the same device and slaps the pile in real time. For 2 to 4 players.
# Turning cards
- All 52 cards are dealt face down, one pile for each player. Nobody looks at their cards.
- In turn, each player turns the top card of their pile onto the pile in the middle.
- An ace, king, queen or jack demands tribute: the next player turns cards until they turn one of those too, with 4 tries for an ace, 3 for a king, 2 for a queen and 1 for a jack. If they manage it, the next player owes tribute in turn; if not, the player of the face card wins the whole pile, puts it under their own and plays next.
# Slapping
- Anyone may slap the pile at any moment. A double, two cards of the same rank one on the other, wins the pile for the first player to slap, who plays next.
- In the sandwich game (the picker's card with a king, a four and a king), a sandwich counts too: two cards of the same rank with one card between them.
- A wrong slap costs a card: your top card goes face down under the middle pile.
- A player with no cards left turns no more cards but may still slap to come back. A wrong slap with no card to give puts them out.
# The end of the game
The player who wins all the cards wins the game.
# Playing on the device
Lay the device flat between the players. The table is cut into one zone for each player: half the screen each with two, the top half split in two with three, a quarter each with four. With face to face on, the zones across the table are turned to face their players; each player's bar is on their side. Your zone shows your pile, your token and how many cards you have. When it is your turn your pile glows: tap it to turn a card. To slap, tap anywhere else in your zone. Each dot above a pile is a try left to pay a tribute. The first slap the device sees wins: a right slap lights the zone in gold, a wrong one in red. When a tribute is not paid, the pile waits a moment so that a last double can still be slapped, then goes to the face card's player, who may also tap their zone to take it at once. Everyone can touch the screen at the same time, each in their own zone. Undo takes back the last action.
