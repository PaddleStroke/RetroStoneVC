Piquet
# The aim
Rubicon piquet, the classic French game for two: over six deals, score more than your opponent, and at least 100 points.
# The cards
32 cards, ace high down to 7. The deal alternates; the player who does not deal is the elder hand. 12 cards each; the other 8 are the talon.
# The exchange
The elder throws 1 to 5 cards and takes as many from the top of the talon. Then the younger may throw up to as many as are left and take them.
# The declarations
Each kind scores only for the player who holds the better one; equal ones score nothing. Each player declares the best their hand holds, before the first lead.
- Carte blanche: twelve cards dealt without a king, queen or jack: 10.
- The point: the most cards in one suit (if equal, the higher value: ace 11, king, queen and jack 10, the others their number): 1 point per card.
- Sequences: three or more cards following in one suit. The longest wins (if equal, the higher top card) and its holder scores all their sequences: 3 cards 3, 4 cards 4, 5 to 8 cards 15 to 18.
- Sets: three or four cards of one rank, 10 or higher. Four beat three, then the higher rank wins; the winner scores all their sets: four 14, three 3.
# Playing
The elder leads. Follow suit if you can; there are no trumps. The higher card of the suit led wins and leads next. Leading a 10 or higher scores 1; winning your opponent's lead with a 10 or higher scores 1; the last trick 1 more. Most tricks (7 or more) score 10; all 12 (capot) 40 instead.
# Pique and repique
- Repique: 30 points from the declarations alone (in order: carte blanche, point, sequences, sets) before the opponent scores anything: 60 more.
- Pique: the elder reaching 30 with declarations and play (not most tricks nor capot) before the younger scores anything: 30 more.
# The end of the game
After six deals the higher total wins. A loser under 100 is rubiconed: the winner scores both totals plus 100; otherwise the difference plus 100. Equal totals: a draw.
